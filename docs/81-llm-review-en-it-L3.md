# en-it L3 — LLM review

The review that clears each en-it L3 wave to ship, written in the same change that authors it
(`CLAUDE.md`, "Ship `verified: true` in the authoring change"). The **native-speaker gate is a
separate, stricter bar and stays unmet**: every section below ends in open questions for a native
pass, and no later wave may close one of them by rewriting a shipped module.

Open questions are numbered as a fresh en-it L3 chain from 1. `docs/65`'s L2 chain is closed and is
not continued here.

## Wave 1 — L3-M1, L3-M2 (#475)

Authored against the briefs written by #466 and the decisions recorded in `docs/73`, and reviewed
against the REAL cumulative index rather than against the briefs' account of it: **642 surfaces
through L2-M10, `maxSpan` 3**, growing to 683 after M1 and 717 after M2. A strict `npm run build`
emits `en-it: 22 modules (L1-M1..M10, L2-M1..M10, L3-M1..M2)` with `CONTENT 202/202 ok`. `maxSpan`
holds at 3, and it is worth saying why it did not have to move: the level's longest new key,
`mi occupo di`, is exactly three tokens, so the span L2 already paid for covers it.

### L3-M1 "Your day, in detail" — a verb marked twice, and a learner who marks it once

The vocabulary of a routine is L1-M4's and the tense is L1's; what makes this a level rather than a
longer M4 is one fact about the verb. **An Italian reflexive is marked twice and an English one is
not marked at all.** The pronoun stands in front of the verb and changes with the person, and the
ending changes with it — `mi sveglio`, `ti svegli`, `si sveglia`. English says "I get up" with
nothing in that slot, so the Italian pronoun reads as a spare word and gets dropped, and three of
the module's ten mistake plates are exactly that drop. It is stated in rule 0 in the terms a learner
needs rather than in the terms a grammar uses: not "myself", not optional, part of the verb.

The second decision is the one that stops a learner building two half-rules. Every reflexive pronoun
in the module is a word the course already owns doing a second job — `mi` and `ti` are L2-M5's, `si`
is L2-M4's, `ci` is L2-M6's `ci vediamo` — and rule 1 says so out loud, along with the warning that
the same `mi` will be an OBJECT in L3-M5. One family of little words with two jobs. A course that
lets a learner meet `mi` twice without saying that has taught two families, and the second meeting
is only two modules away.

**`mai` needs `non`**, and Italian requires the second negative where English forbids it. The shape
was shipped in L2 (`non c'era nessuno`) and never named; here it is a rule with the honest half
attached — bare `mai` means "ever", so `Mangio mai a casa` is not a weaker negative but a different
sentence, which is why the plate can say the `non` is compulsory without hedging.

The frequency set costs nothing but placement, and rule 3 says exactly that: `di solito` and
`qualche volta` frame a clause and go in front; `spesso`, `sempre`, `raramente` and `mai` colour the
verb and sit behind it. Nothing agrees and nothing changes shape. Naming the whole price is worth a
rule of its own in a course whose last two levels taught agreement everywhere — a learner braced for
Italian to bend something will go looking for the bend.

The sequencing spine is nearly free: `prima` and `dopo` are L2-M4's direction words doing a time
job, `poi` is L1-M10's, and `infine` is the only new word in it. Its mistake plate is the best small
thing in the module. `finalmente` is relief — at last! — and `infine` is the last item of a list;
English "finally" collapses the two and hands the learner the wrong one. L2-M10's `alla fine` says
how a story ENDED, and the row points at it rather than colliding with it.

### The seam the brief got wrong, and the module that came out of it

The M1 brief listed `mi alzo`, `ti alzi` and `si alza` among its fresh keys. The real index says
otherwise: **all three are L1-M4's**, opened at `L1-M4-S02`, where a first-level routine module
taught getting up without naming the system under it. The correction is recorded in the brief's
INDEX SEAM note, and the authoring wave took it literally rather than working around it:

- the system opens on the four verbs the course does NOT own — `svegliarsi`, `lavarsi`, `vestirsi`,
  `riposarsi` — so every row a learner can reach is a row that teaches something;
- `alzarsi` is paid for only where the course owns no cell: `ci alziamo` and `si alzano` at S09,
  with `vi alzate` in the `forms`;
- `mi alzo` is still SHOWN, at S02 and twice in the pool, and resolves to L1-M4. That is the right
  outcome rather than a compromise — the learner who taps it gets the row that first taught it;
- and the teaching a row would have carried — that the pronoun repeats on every verb and cannot be
  said once for a whole list — moved into S02's TRAP. `Mi alzo, mi lavo e mi vesto` against English's
  "I get up, wash and dress" is a better home for it than a note nobody would ever open.

That last move is the general lesson and it is worth writing down for the waves that follow: when a
key is spent, the teaching is not dropped, it is RELOCATED — into a trap, into a rule, or onto a
sentence the module can own. A row nobody reaches teaches nothing at all.

The same note caught `ogni`. `ogni giorno` is L1-M4's WHOLE two-token key, so bare `ogni` only
reaches a learner when something other than `giorno` follows it — which is why S08 is `Ogni lunedì
mi riposo a casa` and not a sentence about every day. It is writable at all because L2-M6 opened the
bare `lunedì` while L1-M4 owns only `il lunedì` as a whole; and the trap that falls out of that pair
is real Italian rather than an index artefact. `il lunedì` states Mondays as a rule and `ogni
lunedì` counts them one by one, and `ogni` will not take the article that `il lunedì` is made of.

### L3-M2 "Work and study" — the article that vanishes, then comes straight back

Rule 0 is the sentence a work module says most often, and it runs against English twice in a row:
`sono insegnante` takes no article at all, and `sono un bravo insegnante` takes one the moment an
adjective arrives. English inserts an article in both halves, so a learner who only learns the first
half over-corrects into the second — `sono bravo insegnante` — and S02's plate is exactly that. Two
sentences, two plates, and the pair has to be taught together or the module teaches a habit that
breaks on the next adjective.

The prepositions are the module's honest part. There is no rule under `lavoro in un ufficio`,
`lavoro per una ditta`, `lavoro da casa`, `studio all'università`, and rule 1 says there is none
rather than inventing one: four items, a closed list, and asking why will not help. The pair that
earns the most is `per` against `in` — `per` names who employs you and `in` names where you are, so
both sentences are correct Italian about different facts, and S04's trap is the only place a learner
will ever be told which one they just said.

`in ufficio` and `in un ufficio` sit four sentences apart in one module on purpose (S03 and S07).
Bare, it means "at work"; with `un`, it means inside some particular room. Shipping only one of them
would leave the other looking like a typo the first time a learner meets it in the wild.

`devo` earns its place by contrast with a word L1 already shipped. `vorrei` is one frozen cell of a
tense the course has not opened — L3-M4's — and `devo` is a whole present tense that conjugates like
any other. Rule 3 says that out loud, which is what keeps L1's debt visible instead of letting a
learner file two useful words as the same kind of thing.

`mi occupo di` is the module's clitic decision and it is made the way `docs/73` §4 said it would be:
ONE key, three tokens, with both the pronoun and the preposition inside it. The learner is never
shown a bare `mi` here that would resolve to L2-M5's object pronoun, and the phrase is taught as
what it is — an Italian verb that owns its preposition, where English's owns a different one.

### The second seam correction: two modals the course had already bought

The M2 brief chartered `devo`, `posso` and `voglio` as a fresh trio. The index says `posso` is
L2-M1's, opened at S04 for asking permission, and `voglio` is L1-M3's, opened at S01 for wanting
something. **Only `devo` was free.** The correction was taken as literally as M1's: no row was
opened for the other two, both are still shown — `Non posso lavorare, devo studiare per un esame` at
S08 and `voglio una laurea` at S09 — and the law that covers the family lives in rule 2, with S08's
trap naming `posso` as L2-M1's may-I word doing its plain job here.

That is the right shape for the finding rather than a workaround. The bare-infinitive law is a fact
about three verbs and not about one of them: a rules entry states it once for all three, where three
word rows would have stated it three times and two of the three would have been unreachable.

`da` took the same treatment for a different reason. The course has never owned it bare — its only
appearance anywhere is inside L2-M10's `da bambino` — so `da casa` is indexed WHOLE and bare `da`
stays unspent for whichever module first needs it as a preposition in its own right. The trap does
the rest of the work: `a casa` is going or being home and `da casa` is working out of it, one letter
apart.

### Two rows that ride on a sentence that cannot hold them

First occurrence wins INSIDE a module as well as across the level, and a work module is where that
bites, because every sentence is about a profession. S01 spends `insegnante` on its own display, so
S02 — `Sono un bravo insegnante di italiano` — opens no row for any word in its display at all: its
single row is `medico`, which its own variations show (`È un bravo medico`). S07 does the same with
`capo`, opened beside `riunione` and shown only in a variation.

Nothing about that is a dodge. A row teaches a surface the module SHOWS; it has never had to be a
surface that this particular display shows, and the emitted index confirms both rows landed where
they were meant to (`medico` → `L3-M2-S02`, `capo` → `L3-M2-S07`). The alternative was a second
`insegnante` row, which the duplicate check would have caught and which no learner would ever have
reached.

### The ratchet

`tools/shown-surfaces.test.ts` held at **en-it 17** across both modules, and this is the first
authoring wave in the milestone with **no finding at all** — not one shown-but-untaught surface in
either module. That is not luck and it is not a weaker check: it is what happens when the seam notes

would each have produced a finding — `mi alzo`, `ogni giorno`, `posso`/`voglio` — were caught while
they were still a sentence in a brief.

### Open questions for the native pass

1. **`di solito` at the front** (M1-S01). Confirm the fronted position is the neutral one, and that
   `Mi sveglio di solito alle sette` is not what a speaker reaches for first.
2. **`svegliarsi` against `alzarsi`** (M1-S01, S02). Confirm the two verbs are kept apart in ordinary
   speech, and that a learner who says `mi sveglio` for both is heard as saying something slightly
   different rather than something wrong.
3. **Adverb placement** (M1-S05, S07). The module says `spesso` sits after the verb while
   `raramente` may open a clause or follow it. Confirm both, and that `Spesso vado al bar` really
   does set up a contrast a listener then waits for.
4. **`qualche volta` against `a volte`** (M1-S06). The module teaches only `qualche volta` and never
   mentions `a volte`. Confirm it is at least as ordinary as the word it leaves out.
5. **`ogni lunedì` against `il lunedì`** (M1-S08). Confirm both are everyday for a weekly habit, and
   that the difference is the counting/rule one the trap draws rather than a register difference.
6. **`infine` against `finalmente`** (M1-S10). Confirm `infine` is what a speaker says at the end of
   a spoken list, and that `alla fine` is not quietly doing that job in speech instead.
7. **`non … mai`, and bare `mai`** (M1, rule 2). Confirm `Mangio mai a casa` is heard as an error
   rather than as an emphatic or old-fashioned negative.
8. **`fare colazione`** (M1-S03). Confirm the article-free phrase, and that `prendere il caffè` is
   not what most people would actually say about their own morning.
9. **A bare profession** (M2, rule 0 and S01). Confirm `Sono un insegnante` is wrong rather than
   merely marked, and that the article returns with ANY adjective and not only an evaluating one.
10. **`ditta`** (M2-S04). The module says `ditta` is the word in the conversation and `azienda` the
    word on the letterhead. This is the wave's most exposed vocabulary claim — confirm it, including
    whether `ditta` now sounds small or dated to a younger speaker.
11. **`per` against `in` for an employer** (M2-S04). Confirm both are ordinary, and that the
    contract/building split is how a speaker actually hears the two sentences.
12. **`in ufficio` against `in un ufficio`** (M2-S03, S07). Confirm the bare phrase means "at work"
    generally and that the two are not interchangeable in ordinary speech.
13. **`il mio capo` of a woman** (M2-S07). Confirm the masculine article stands for a female boss in
    ordinary speech, and whether `la capa` is now heard often enough to be worth naming.
14. **`mi occupo di stipendi`** (M2-S10). Confirm the bare plural after `di` with no article, and
    that the phrase is what somebody actually says about their own job rather than a written one.

## Wave 2 — L3-M3, L3-M4, L3-M5 (#484)

Authored against the same briefs and `docs/73`, and reviewed against the REAL cumulative index at
each step rather than against the briefs' account of it: **717 surfaces through L3-M2**, growing to
**739 after M3, 754 after M4 and 765 after M5**. `maxSpan` holds at **3** across all three, and the
three modules are what proves the span was priced correctly rather than luckily: `mi sembra che`,
`al posto tuo`, `li ho visti` and `le ho parlato` are each exactly three tokens, and none of them
could have been taught at all if L2 had settled for two. A strict `npm run content:build` emits
`en-it: 25 modules (L1-M1..M10, L2-M1..M10, L3-M1..M5)` with `CONTENT 209/209 ok`.

The wave's three grammars are the three `docs/57` withheld and `docs/73` §2 chartered, and they
arrive in the order a learner can carry them: a mood on one trigger, a tense that was already in
their mouth, and a set of little words the course could not spell until now.

### L3-M3 "Opinions with reasons" — a mood opened four cells wide

The decision `docs/73` §2 made is the one the module lives or dies on: the congiuntivo opens as
FOUR CELLS on ONE trigger family, and not as a system. `penso che`, `credo che` and `mi sembra che`
take `sia`, `abbia`, `possa`, `vada`, and rule 0 says in its own words that the mood as a system —
every verb, every tense, every other trigger — is L4's. The cost is worth paying here because the
alternative is not silence: an English speaker who is never told will say `penso che è vero` for two
more levels, and that sentence is heard at once. Rule 1 is the module's one loud thing and it says
exactly why the error is invisible from inside English — the English verb does not move after "I
think", so nothing in the sentence being translated warns you that the Italian one must.

**The escape hatch carries as much weight as the rule, and it is the same word L1 already shipped.**
`penso di` has been L1-M9's whole two-token key since the first level, taught there with an explicit
promise that `penso che` "needs a mood not taught here". M3 keeps that promise and then explains it:
with the SAME subject in both halves Italian does not use `che` at all, so `Penso di avere ragione`
is a correct, ordinary, mood-free sentence for half of what a learner wants to say. S06's mistake
plate is `Penso che io abbia ragione` — grammatical, and nobody says it. No row was opened for
`penso di`; the key is L1-M9's and stays there, and rule 2 carries the teaching, which is the
relocation lesson wave 1 wrote down.

`anche se` is the counterweight and it sits four sentences from `penso che sia` on purpose. Rule 3
states the reason plainly: the mood belongs to the TRIGGER and not to the idea of a subordinate
clause. A learner who has just met the mood will reach for it everywhere, and `anche se` is the
first place that goes wrong. S08 puts `perché` in the same seat for the same reason.

Two phrases in the module are not verbs, and rule 4 says so once for both. `secondo me` needs no
verb at all — two words and then the claim, which makes it the cheapest useful thing in the module —
and it is indexed WHOLE precisely so bare `me` is not spent. `sono d'accordo` is an ADJECTIVE phrase
carried by `essere` and never a verb, its apostrophe inside one straight-marked key. The softening
rule (5) refuses to grade the two languages against each other: Italian marks a claim as an opinion
in the first two words and states the claim plainly after; hedging the claim itself reads as
evasive. Neither language is the polite one, and S10's trap says that rather than implying it.

### The seam M3 got right, and the one row that is deliberately unreachable

`vada` is the only re-teach in the whole wave, and it is one on purpose. `tools/check-shown.ts`
reports it — L2-M4 owns the key, where `Vada sempre dritto` taught the polite imperative — so a
learner tapping `vada` during practice is shown L2-M4's note and not M3's. The row stays anyway,
because Sentence Detail renders each sentence's OWN deconstruction and S05's page is where the fact
belongs: **the polite imperative IS this mood**, which is why one form does both jobs. The teaching
that the index cannot reach is duplicated in S05's trap, so nothing depends on the unreachable note.
This is the one place in the wave where a row was kept knowing the resolver would shadow it.

The rest of the seam list was exactly right. `secondo me`, `sono d'accordo` and `anche se` are all
indexed whole, and the bare `me`, `accordo` and — the one that mattered — `se` all stayed unspent
for M4, which needed `se` two modules later and got it clean.

One softener the brief named was left out. The brief's softening list reads "secondo me, direi, non
sono sicuro, forse", but `direi` is a condizionale cell and the INDEX SEAM does not charter it.
Shipping it in M3 would have planted a fourth frozen conditional a module before M4's whole job is
to show that the frozen ones were a tense all along, so it was dropped rather than smuggled in;
`forse`, `magari` and `non sono sicuro` carry the softening instead. Q19 below asks whether the
module reads thin without it.

### L3-M4 "If and then" — the tense that was already in the learner's mouth

The module's thesis is `docs/73` §2's, and the index made it **stronger than the brief thought**.
The brief says `vorrei` "was shipped at L1-M3 as a frozen politeness and never explained" and lists
`potrei`, `potresti`, `dovrei`, `dovresti`, `sarebbe` and `verrei` as fresh keys. The real index
says **`potresti` and `potrebbe` are L2-M1's**, opened together at `L2-M1-S03` for asking a favour,
each noted there as "a conditional, and one frozen cell of it — like L1-M3's vorrei". So the course
has shipped **three** frozen conditional cells across two levels, not one, and every one of them was
labelled a conditional at the time with no tense behind the label.

The correction was taken the way wave 1 took its two: no row was opened for `potresti` or
`potrebbe`, both are still SHOWN — `Potresti parlare con il capo domani?` at S05's variations — and
the law that covers all of them lives in rule 1, which names all three by module. That is a better
finding than the brief's version, because three cells already in a learner's mouth is a far more
convincing proof that the endings are learnable than one is. Rule 2 states the endings once (`-ei`,
`-esti`, `-ebbe`) and says out loud that the tense opens on five verbs and no more.

Rule 0 exists to say there is no trap, which after M3 is a real service. `Se piove, resto a casa`
lines up with English word for word, and a learner braced by the congiuntivo will invent a bend that
is not there. The one honest difference is small and is S02's whole lesson: Italian puts a plain
present where English insists on "we'll go".

**The counterfactual is exactly one frame, and the honest fact about it is a rule and not a
footnote.** `Se avessi tempo, verrei anche io` is the textbook sentence and S08 teaches it; rule 4
then says, at the same volume, that spoken Italian very often puts the plain imperfetto in BOTH
halves — `Se avevo tempo, venivo` — and that this is ordinary speech rather than an error to correct
in somebody. `docs/73` §5 asked for it in `usage` and S08's `usage` carries it too, because the
sentence page is where a learner meets the frame. A course that denies what a learner hears stops
being believed, and this is the wave's clearest example of paying that.

Advice is the job line and rule 5 is where the delta actually bites: English's "you should" is one
word to a friend and to an employee, and Italian's `dovresti` and `devi` are not interchangeable.
S05's mistake plate is `Devi parlare con il capo domani` — grammatical, and an order.

`al posto tuo` is indexed WHOLE as the seam required, and its trap is the small good thing in the
module: English builds "if I were you" out of a past tense and Italian builds it out of a NOUN, so
there is nothing inside the phrase to conjugate. The canonical continuation of it is a first-person
conditional of an ordinary verb (`Io al posto tuo resterei a casa`), which this module cannot write
because the tense is open on five verbs only — so S06 runs `vorrei restare` instead, which is both
correct and, usefully, `vorrei` doing tense duty in a real conditional sentence rather than a polite
one. Q21 asks a native whether the substitution reads naturally.

`resto`, `tempo`, `soldi` and `subito` are the module's ordinary vocabulary, each earning its row:
`tempo` because it is time and weather at once, `soldi` because it is plural in Italian and singular
in English and takes its article besides, `subito` because it is stressed on the first syllable and
means now rather than soon.

### L3-M5 "What someone said" — three little words the course could not spell until now

`docs/73` §3 predicted this module's whole shape and it paid exactly as predicted. `la` and `le` are
L1-M1's ARTICLE keys forever, so the object clitics are taught as **multi-token surfaces welded to
their verb**: `l'ho vista`, `li ho visti`, `le ho parlato`, `ne ho` — one key each, elision inside,
straight apostrophe, answering for nothing else. Rule 3 says why in the terms a learner needs: this
course never asks you to tell two identically-spelled words apart on sight, because the phrase is
the unit. `lo`, `li`, `mi` and `ti` all stay L2-M5's rows and no row here reopens one.

**The agreement rule and its LIMIT are one rule, and S10 is one sentence containing both halves.**
`Ho visto Anna ieri, e l'ho vista anche oggi` — the object after the verb leaves the participle
alone, the same object in front of it as a clitic moves it. Position is the whole rule. Rule 4 then
closes the two doors a learner walks through next: `le ho parlato` never agrees whoever `le` is,
because it is indirect, and L1-M5's `essere` agreement is a separate law with its own reason. S08's
mistake plate is `L'ho parlata`, which is the exact over-generalisation `docs/73` warned would agree
every participle in the account.

The reporting frame itself is the cheap half and rule 0 says so: English backshifts too, so the move
is free and only the choice of past is new — and that past is L2-M10's imperfetto in a second seat,
with nothing opened for it. The interference is one step further in: rule 1 names the switch English
makes invisibly, pouring "that" after every verb of saying and then quietly using "if" for a
question. `Mi ha chiesto che avevo tempo` is the module's predicted wrong sentence and S03's plate.
The `se` doing that job is M4's key, and M4's note was written to be true of both seats before M5
was authored rather than after.

A request is not a clause at all — `Mi ha detto di aspettare` — and rule 2 points at the `di` a
learner has now met three times in three modules doing the same job: L1-M9's `penso di`, M3's escape
hatch, and this. `aspettare` is tagged `interference` for swallowing English's "for".

`ne` gets a rule of its own (5) because English simply has no word for it and therefore no habit to
correct — only an absence. `Ho due` is not a sentence in Italian, and S09 sets the antecedent up
inside the same line (`Vuoi i biglietti? Ne ho due`) so the "of them" has something to be of.

### The seam M5's brief got wrong: two backshifted cells that cannot be built

The M5 brief lists `veniva`, `abitava` and `voleva` among its fresh keys and writes its worked
example as `dice che viene → ha detto che veniva`. Two of those three cannot be authored against
this index:

- **`abitava` needs a lexeme the course does not own at all.** `abitare` appears nowhere in en-it
  through L3-M4 — not an infinitive, not a present cell. Teaching an imperfetto of a verb whose
  present a learner has never seen would teach a shape with nothing under it. S04 says
  `Mi ha chiesto dove lavoravo` instead, on **L2-M10's own `lavoravo`**, which is precisely what the
  brief's own paragraph asked for: the imperfetto in a second seat, nothing new opened for it.
- **`veniva` is a cell of a verb the course owns only in the conditional.** After M4, `venire`
  exists here as `verrei` and nothing else — no present, no infinitive — so `dice che viene` is not
  a sentence this course can write, and its backshift would be a form with no base. It was dropped;
  S02 backshifts `voleva` instead, whose present (`voglio`, `vuoi`, `vuole`) the learner has owned
  since L1-M3.

Only `voleva` of the three survived, and one further cell was added where the module genuinely
needed it: `tornavo` (with `tornava` in its `forms`), the imperfetto of L1-M10's `torno`, so S04 can
report two questions in one line. The general shape of the finding is wave 1's: **a brief may name a
cell, but only the index can say whether the verb under it exists.** Every other cell in the module
— `era`, `aveva`, `avevo`, `lavoravo`, `andava` — is L2-M10's, shown and not re-taught.

`visto` is the one participle taught bare, and it is taught for the sake of the limit rather than
the rule: without it S10 could not show a participle that does NOT agree, and rule 4 would be half a
law. `vista` and `visti` are never keys of their own — they live inside `l'ho vista` and
`li ho visti` — so the bare row and the welded phrases do not compete.

### The ratchet

`tools/shown-surfaces.test.ts` holds at **en-it 17** across all three modules, and
`npm run content:shown` reports **`clean — every shown surface resolves`** for each of M3, M4 and M5
with **one** re-teach in the whole wave (`vada`, deliberate, above) and none at all in M4 or M5.
That is two consecutive waves with no shown-but-untaught finding on this course.

It is worth naming what produced it, because it was not care at the keyboard. Every sentence,
variation and pool item in these three modules was written against the emitted index, and four facts
that would each have produced a finding were caught before a display was written rather than after:
`potresti` and `potrebbe` already being L2-M1's, `abitare` not existing in the course at all,
`venire` having no present, and `alla` never having been taught bare (only `alla fine`, whole) —
which is why S07 reads `Li ho visti al bar` and not `alla fermata`. The check that catches these
costs seconds; the module that ships without it costs a sweep.

### Open questions for the native pass

15. **`mi sembra che` against `mi sembra`** (M3-S04). The module teaches the full trigger and shows
    the bare `Mi sembra caro` as a variation. Confirm both are everyday, and that `mi sembra che` is

16. **`credo` against `penso`** (M3-S02, S03). The module says `credo` is "a shade more considered,

17. **`non credo che vada bene`** (M3-S05). Confirm this is the ordinary spoken no to a plan, and

18. **`magari` against `forse`** (M3-S09). The module calls `magari` the warmer of the two and gives
    `Magari!` as "if only". Confirm a bare `Magari hai ragione` is not heard as the wish reading.
19. **The softener the module left out** (M3, rule 5). `direi` was dropped so as not to plant a

    me`, `forse`, `magari`, `non sono sicuro` — is enough to sound like a speaker rather than a
    textbook, and say where `direi` would have been missed.
20. **`sono d'accordo` with a reason** (M3-S07, S08). Confirm `anche se` is what a speaker reaches
    for when conceding, and that `pur essendo` and friends are genuinely a register above this.
21. **`Io al posto tuo, vorrei restare`** (M4-S06). The canonical continuation is a first-person
    conditional of the ordinary verb (`resterei`), which this module cannot write. Confirm the
    `vorrei` substitution reads as natural advice and not as a learner working around a gap.
22. **The spoken counterfactual** (M4, rule 4 and S08 `usage`). This is the wave's most exposed
    claim. Confirm `Se avevo tempo, venivo` is ordinary unremarkable speech across regions and
    generations rather than regionally marked or heard as uneducated — the module tells a learner
    not to correct it in anybody.
23. **`dovresti` against `devi`** (M4-S05). Confirm the advice/order split is as sharp as rule 5
    says, and that `devi` to a friend really does land as an instruction rather than as warmth.
24. **`Sarebbe meglio`** (M4-S07). Confirm it is the everyday impersonal advice it is presented as,
    and that a bare `Meglio tornare presto` is not what most speakers would actually say.
25. **`subito`** (M4-S10). Confirm it means now rather than soon in ordinary use, and that
    `dovresti chiamare subito` is not a stronger instruction than the `dovresti` frame suggests.
26. **`le ho parlato` of a man** (M5-S08). The module teaches `le` as "to her" only. Confirm that
    leaving `gli` (to him) out is survivable at this rung rather than a hole a learner falls into on
    their first phone call.
27. **Participle agreement in speech** (M5, rule 4). Confirm `l'ho vista` and `li ho visti` really
    are agreed in ordinary spoken Italian — the written rule is not in doubt, the spoken habit is —
    and that a speaker who says `l'ho visto` of a woman is heard as wrong rather than as casual.
28. **`ne ho due`** (M5-S09). Confirm the `ne` is genuinely compulsory in the spoken answer, and
    that no register drops it the way the module says English does.
29. **`Mi ha chiesto se`** (M5-S03). Confirm the reported yes/no question is `se` and never `che` in
    speech as well as in writing, including after `chiedere` in the passato prossimo.
30. **The backshift a speaker actually uses** (M5, rule 0). Confirm `Ha detto che era stanco` is
    what is said, and that the present is not routinely left in place in casual retelling
    (`ha detto che è stanco`) — the same honesty question rule 4 of M4 answers for conditionals.

## Wave 3 — L3-M6..M10 (#551)

The wave that closes the level. Authored against the briefs printed by `npm run content:kit` and,
as in both earlier waves, against the REAL emitted index rather than against the briefs' account of
it: **765 surfaces through L3-M5**, growing to 830 after M6, 858 after M7, 899 after M8, 925 after
M9 and **958 after M10**. A strict `npm run content:build` emits
`en-it: 30 modules (L1-M1..M10, L2-M1..M10, L3-M1..M10)` with `CONTENT 214/214 ok`. `maxSpan` holds
at **3** for the fifth wave running: the longest new keys of this wave — `mi dà fastidio`,
`mi fanno male` — are exactly three tokens, so the span L2 paid for still covers the level.

### L3-M6 "Feelings in depth" — one ending apart, and a whole different sentence

The module's job is a single pair of endings, and the brief was right that it is a meaning
difference rather than a stylistic one. `sono annoiato` is what happened to you; `è noioso` is what
the thing is. Rule 0 states it in the terms a learner needs — **`-ato` is done TO you, `-ante` or
`-oso` DOES it** — and S02 carries the mistake plate the brief asked for, because `Sono noioso` is a
grammatical Italian sentence that says something a learner never means.

The brief's note 5 asked whether each `-ante`/`-ato` pair is one row with both shapes in `forms` or
two rows. The emitted index settles it as **two rows in every case**, and for a mechanical reason
rather than a semantic one: the two shapes have **different agreement paradigms**. `interessato`
takes four endings because it agrees with a person; `interessante` takes two because `-ante`
adjectives do not mark gender. One row cannot hold two paradigms without implying that
`interessanta` exists. `annoiato`/`noioso` are two words outright (`annoiare` against `noia`), and
`stanco`/`stancante` needed only one row here because **`stanco` is already L1-M2's**.

The having-and-giving family is the second half of the module and it is where the course's own
shapes pay off: `ho paura`, `ho voglia di`, `mi fa piacere` and `mi dà fastidio` are all indexed
WHOLE, so **bare `mi` stays L2-M5's** exactly as the brief required. `mi dà fastidio` earned rule 4
on its own, because it agrees with the thing that annoys you (`mi danno fastidio i rumori`) and is
therefore a third member of the `mi piace` family rather than a new law. `mi fa piacere` was taken
whole as well, though the brief named it only in note 3 and not in the seam list: leaving bare
`piacere` as a key would have put a noun in front of a learner who knows only the verb.

### L3-M7 "Body and health" — three seams the brief got wrong, and the one it flagged

This module needed more index corrections than any other in the level, and all of them were found
before a display was written.

- **`medico` is not fresh.** Note 5 lists it among the new keys; **L3-M2 owns it**. No row was
  written and the word resolves back to M2, where a learner met it in a sentence about work.
- **`piedi` is not fresh.** Note 5 lists it too; **L2-M8 owns both `piede` and `piedi`**. No row.
- **`mi fa male` is not "L2-M8's row".** Note 1 says the module points back at it. There is no
  `mi fa male` key in the index at all: the phrase resolves token by token — `mi` from L2-M5, `fa`
  from L1-M6, `male` from L2-M8 — so pointing back means pointing at three rows in three different
  modules. `mi fanno male` WAS free as a three-token span, and it was taken whole, which is where
  the agreement note now lives. This is the cleanest outcome for the brief's intent: the singular is
  genuinely not re-taught, and the plural gets the row it needs.
- **The one conditional the brief flagged resolves to fresh.** Note 5 says `da` is new "only if the
  emitted index still has it free". It is free: the index carries `da bambino` and `da bambina`
  (L2-M10, whole) and no bare `da`. It got a row, and it is the module's best delta — one word for
  English's "for" and "since", with the verb staying in the present.
- **`giorni` had to be taught.** `da tre giorni` cannot be written without it, and L1-M4 owns only
  the whole keys `ogni giorno` and `tutti i giorni`, never the bare noun.
- **`destra` is not a key** — only `a destra` (L2-M4, whole). A planned variation `la gamba destra`
  was rewritten to `la gamba da ieri`, which does more work anyway.

The one place the module spends a row on grammar rather than on vocabulary is `braccio`, whose
plural `le braccia` changes gender. It is taught as a pair to learn rather than as a rule, because
it is not one.

### L3-M8 "Money and paperwork" — the impersonal at length, and one elision that nearly cost a finding

The brief's note 1 asked for `si` shown at length rather than opened a second time, and that is what
shipped: `si paga`, `si può`, `si vendono` and `si firma` are all indexed WHOLE, so **L2-M4's bare
`si` keeps its row**. The plural plate the brief asked for is rule 1 and S03, and it is the only
mechanical rule in the module: `si vendono i biglietti`, because the verb counts what comes after
it.

Two seam facts are worth recording:

- **`l'affitto` does not fold to `affitto`.** The elided article is part of the token, so a display
  reading `pagare l'affitto` leaves `l'affitto` unresolved unless it is in the row's `forms`. It is
  now. This is the same class of fact the M10 brief flagged for `all'inizio`, and it is worth
  stating as a general one for this course: **any en-it row whose noun elides needs the elided
  surface in `forms`.**
- **`firma` the noun and `si firma` the impersonal do not collide**, because they fold to different
  keys. They are therefore two rows, and `firmare`'s `forms` deliberately exclude `firma` to keep it
  that way — listing it there would have opened the same key twice inside one module, which is the
  defect `npm run content:shown` fails on.

One small correction to notes 3 and 5: they say `devo` and `posso` "stay M2's". `devo` is L3-M2's;
**`posso` is L2-M1's**, alongside `scusi` and `senta`. Nothing turns on it — neither took a row —
but the brief's attribution is wrong.

The module speaks the polite address throughout, per `docs/57` §1, and **`Lei` appears in no
display**: the register is carried by `ha` rather than `hai`, by `scusi` and `firmi`, and by the
impersonal itself. That is the course's shipped practice and this module did not need to break it.

### L3-M9 "Festivals and everyday culture" — the relative clause, and where it could not be written

Note 1 asks for the relative job of `che` to be "a third note on that row, not a third row". **This
module cannot do that.** L1-M5 owns `che`, a level never edits a file below it, and a `che` row here
would be an unreachable re-teach — the learner would be shown L1-M5's note, not this one. The law
went into **module rule 0** instead, which names L1-M5 and L3-M3 explicitly so the three jobs read
as one word rather than three. `cui` is the only new pronoun, exactly as the brief said.

Four corrections to notes 2, 4 and 5:

- **`primo` is not fresh.** Note 5 hedges it ("where they are not owned"); **L2-M4 owns it**. No
  row. `il primo gennaio` is written from L2-M4's word and this module's `gennaio`.
- **`quindici` is not fresh either, and the brief could not have known why:** L3-M8, two modules
  earlier in this same wave, teaches it. M9's dates reuse it.
- **The habitual article is not new to the course.** Note 2 presents `la domenica` against
  `domenica` as the module's law, and it is the right law — but L1-M4 already ships **`il lunedì`
  and `il sabato` as whole keys** (and `di mattina`, `di sera`). What is genuinely new here is the
  minimal pair inside one sentence, which is what S07 is. `la domenica` was taken as a fresh whole
  key; bare `domenica` stays L2-M6's.
- **`si mangia` is not one of M8's rows.** Note 4 says to point back at M8 rather than open new
  surfaces. M8 owns `si paga`, `si può`, `si vendono` and `si firma` — not `si mangia`. As it
  happens `si mangia` needed **no row at all**: both tokens already resolve (`si` L2-M4, `mangia`
  L1-M4), which is the cheapest possible way to honour the brief. `si chiude` did need one, because
  `chiude` is untaught.

The festival names each got a row rather than being left to the proper-noun exemption of #61. The
reason is empirical: en-it's shown-but-untaught baseline of 17 **already contains proper nouns**
(`anna` heads the list), so this course's counter does not exempt them, and four untaught festival
names would have pushed the baseline to 21. Lowering a baseline is allowed in the commit that fixes
the content; raising one is not.

### L3-M10 "Your own story" — an account is a vocabulary problem, not a grammar one

The brief is right that nothing is opened here. What it under-counts is how much of an account is
constrained by what the course happens NOT to own, and the corrections are all of that kind.

- **`alla fine` is not fresh.** Note 5 lists it among the new keys; **L2-M10 owns it whole.** No row
  was written and it is used freely — four of the ten accounts end on it. Wave 2's review had
  already found the neighbouring fact, that bare `alla` is untaught.
- **`all'inizio` is fresh and is one key carrying its elision**, exactly as note 5 said. It is the
  only prediction the brief made about this index and it was correct.
- **`sette` is not a key.** L1-M4 owns `alle sette` whole and nothing else, so a planned
  `il treno delle sette` became `il treno per Milano`.
- **`abbiamo` does not exist in this course.** `siamo`, `andiamo`, `facciamo`, `mangiamo`,
  `prendiamo`, `torniamo`, `vediamo` and `dobbiamo` are all taught; the first-person plural of
  `avere` is not. **No account in this module may use a first-person-plural `avere` perfect**, which
  is why every one of the ten is told in the first person singular or the third. This is the single
  fact that shaped the module's prose most, and it is invisible from the brief.

The participles are the honest spend the brief predicted, and the list was cut against the index
rather than written from scratch. Already owned and therefore **not** re-taught: `andato`,
`tornato`, `partito`, `rimasto`, `uscito`, `stato`, `preso`, `perso`, `dimenticato`, `lasciato`,
`chiamato`, `chiesto`, `parlato`, `comprato`, `mangiato`, `bevuto`, `dormito`, `studiato`, and
L3-M5's `visto` and `detto`. Genuinely new, and each because an account needed it: `arrivato`,
`finito`, `cominciato`, `trovato`, `fatto`, `successo`, `venuto`, `capito`, `scritto`, `letto`,
`dovuto`. The same triage applied to the imperfetto — `ero`, `era`, `avevo`, `c'era`, `faceva`,
`andava`, `lavoravo`, `mangiava`, `pioveva`, `tornavo` and `voleva` are all L2-M10's or L3-M5's, and
only **`aspettavo`** (which `mentre` needs) and **`stavo`** (which health needs) were opened.

`mentre` against `appena` is the pair the module is built on and the brief did not name it. `mentre`
takes the imperfetto because something must still be going on for it to be cut into; `appena` takes
the passato prossimo because both of its events are finished. They look like the same kind of word
and they take opposite tenses, which is rule 1 and S04's trap.

### The ratchet

`tools/shown-surfaces.test.ts` holds at **en-it 17** across all five modules — the level closed
without moving it — and `npx vitest run tools/shown-surfaces.test.ts` is **11/11**.
`npm run content:shown` reports **`clean — every shown surface resolves`** for each of M6, M7, M8,
M9 and M10, with **zero re-teaches and zero collisions in the entire wave**. That is three
consecutive waves on this course with no shown-but-untaught finding, and the first of the three with
no re-teach at all.

The method is wave 2's and it is now worth stating as the course's practice rather than as a happy
result: **every display, variation and pool item was checked against the emitted index before it was
written**, and the corrections above are what that check returned. Eight facts each of which would
have produced a finding were caught in advance — `medico` and `piedi` being owned already,
`mi fa male` not existing as a key, `alla fine` and `primo` being owned, `l'affitto` and `sette` not
folding the way an author expects, and `abbiamo` not existing at all. Sixteen more were caught after
a first draft by running `npm run content:shown` on it: `eravamo`, `destra`, `dice`, `venticinque`,
`negozi`, `conosco`, `carta`, `senza`, `solo`, `qual`, `alla`, `cassa`, `niente`, `grave`, `strana`,
`parlavo` and `aspettato` were all shown-but-untaught in a draft, and every one was rewritten around
rather than taught. The check costs seconds and it is the only reason this level closed on its
baseline.
