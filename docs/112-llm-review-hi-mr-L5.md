# LLM review — hi-mr L5 (Idiom and play)

The level's first wave: **L5-M1 "Sayings and idioms"** and **L5-M2 "Humour and teasing"**, authored
together on 2026-09-08 and shipped `verified: true` in the same change, signed
`Claude Opus 5 — LLM review, authorised by repo owner`. `docs/103-hi-mr-L5-brief-decisions.md` is
the brief these were written to; this file is the record of where that brief was right, where it
had gone stale, and what a native pass still has to settle.

Both modules were written against the **real emitted index, not the brief's picture of it**.
`public/content/hi-mr/index/` folded through its deepest file, `L4-M10.json`, and every ownership
claim below is quoted verbatim from `npm run content:owner -- hi-mr …`, whose closing line on
every run in this wave read:

```
809 surfaces owned, folded over 40 modules through L4-M10
```

Both L5 briefs say **"the 39-module index (808 surfaces … through L4-M9)"**. That is one module and
one surface short: L4-M10 shipped after the briefs were written, and the fold is 40 modules and 809
surfaces deep today. Nothing the briefs claimed about a specific surface changed because of it —
every substantive claim in both INDEX SEAM notes survived re-checking — but the count and the
terminal module in both briefs are stale and should not be quoted forward. This is the seam the
authoring law warns about, and it is worth saying plainly that here it was **only** the count.

hi-mr remains the one course of the nine with `maxSpan 1`, so "index this phrase whole" was never
available: every multi-word shape in these two modules — `डोकं खाणे`, `संकटाला तोंड देणे`,
`कान भरणे`, `अति तिथे माती`, `दिसतं तसं नसतं`, `म्हणतात ना`, `काय रे`, `ही गंमत नाही` — is carried
in `rules[]` and in each sentence's `literal`, and every key bought is exactly one token.

## Wave 1 — L5-M1 "Sayings and idioms", L5-M2 "Humour and teasing"

### L5-M1 — Sayings and idioms

The figurative everyday, and when it is used. Ten displays:

1. `तू रोज माझं डोकं खाऊ नकोस`
2. `डोकं खाणे म्हणजे सारखं त्रास देणे`
3. `डोकं खाणे हा एक वाक्प्रचार आहे`
4. `मला संकटाला तोंड द्यावं लागतं`
5. `तू माझे कान भरू नकोस`
6. `अति तिथे माती, ही एक म्हण आहे`
7. `खूप काम करू नकोस, अति तिथे माती म्हणतात ना`
8. `दिसतं तसं नसतं, असं म्हणतात`
9. `देवाच्या कृपेने आणि नशिबाने ते काम झालं`
10. `देवाची इच्छा असती तर किती बरं झालं असतं`

What it teaches, in the order the brief demands it. **Two systems, named apart** (rules 0 and 1):
a `वाक्प्रचार` is a noun plus a light verb with a slot the speaker fills — S01, S02 and S03 walk
`डोकं खाणे` through `खाऊ नकोस`, `खातो` and `खाल्लं` so that the learner watches the verb move and
the noun not move; a `म्हण` is a whole proverb with no slot at all, quoted in exactly its own words
— S06, S07 and S08 show `अति तिथे माती` and `दिसतं तसं नसतं` unchanged across three frames. S03 is
the sentence that names the two categories against each other, and its second variation
(`डोकं खाणे ही म्हण नाही`) is the negative half of the same statement. The line the brief insists
on — that the difference is a slot and not a length — is rule 1's last sentence and S03's
`mistake.why`.

**The figurative reading lives in the sentence, never in the noun** (rule 2). That is not an
aesthetic choice here, it is forced by the index: `डोकं` is L3-M7's and this module cannot own it
(see seam 1). So every figurative sentence carries the literal reading in `literal` — S01's
`तू रोज़ मेरा सिर खा मत`, S04's `मुझे मुसीबत-को मुँह देना पड़ता है` — and the meaning in `glossEn`,
and the gap between those two lines is the lesson.

**The interference the brief asked for, shown as a pair** (rule 3): S05 `कान भरणे` is the idiom that
crosses from Hindi whole (`कान भरना`), and its `trap` says so and then immediately says not to
generalise; S04 `तोंड देणे` is the one that does not cross, with the calque
`मला संकटाचा सामना करावा लागतो` standing as its `mistake`. They sit next to each other on purpose.

**The two deferrals the brief routed here.** `-च्या कृपेने` / `नशिबाने` land in S09 as fused
single-token keys. `काश`, which L4-M3 kept out and named this module for, is **not bought as a row**
— rule 5 and S10 say in as many words that Marathi answers it with a frame,
`… असतं तर किती बरं झालं असतं`, and S10's `mistake` is the bare `काश देवाची इच्छा असती`.

Twenty-five keys bought, exactly at `newWordCap`: `खाऊ`, `खाणे`, `म्हणजे`, `सारखं`, `त्रास`, `देणे`,
`वाक्प्रचार`, `संकटाला`, `तोंड`, `द्यावं`, `लागतं`, `कान`, `भरू`, `अति`, `माती`, `म्हण`, `म्हणी`,
`म्हणतात`, `दिसतं`, `देवाच्या`, `देवाची`, `कृपेने`, `नशिबाने`, `नशीब`, `इच्छा`.

### L5-M2 — Humour and teasing

Banter, irony, and when not to. Ten displays:

1. `अरे रोहन, तू पण ना!`
2. `अगं आई, तू खूप चेष्टा करतेस!`
3. `काय रे, तुला काय कळतं?`
4. `काय गं, तू मला विसरलीस?`
5. `वा, छान काम केलंस!`
6. `मीच तुला सांगितलं होतं`
7. `तेच तर, मला पण तसंच वाटतं`
8. `मी सहज विनोद केला, तो हसला`
9. `बापरे, हे तर खोटं आहे!`
10. `तू मला चिडवू नकोस, ही गंमत नाही`

What it teaches. **The particle-and-vocative layer**, which is where the brief locates Marathi
banter: the gendered vocatives `अरे` / `अगं` (S01, S02) and their tags `रे` / `गं` (S03, S04); the
mock question `काय रे …?` whose question mark is a costume (S03, and rule 2 says the frame *is* the
marking); the ironic-praise frame `वा, छान … केलंस!` (S05), whose two moving parts are `वा` at the
front and the `-स` on the verb — S05's `mistake` is the same line with `केलं` for `केलंस`, which
aims the praise at nobody; and the welded clitic `च` in `मीच`, `तेच`, `तसंच` (S06, S07).

**The gendered vocative is treated as the module's central error, as brief note 3 requires.** It is
the `mistake` on both S01 (`अगं रोहन`) and S02 (`अरे आई`), and it is doubled by the verb: S04 pairs
`गं` with `विसरलीस` and its `mistake` is `काय गं, तू मला विसरलास?` — one half changed and the other
not. `यार` is named as Hindi-marked in S03's `mistake.why` and in rule 4.

**The line against L5-M7 is drawn grammatically, not by degree** (rule 2, and rule 4's last clause):
everything here is *overtly* marked — by a vocative, a particle, or an obviously false claim — and
the listener is meant to see the marking. Nothing in this module means the opposite of its words
without a marker saying so.

**Where it stops.** Rule 5 names the three rooms (a stranger, anyone taking `तुम्ही`, work) and
S10 ships the stop line `ही गंमत नाही` as a fixed sentence, with `usage` restating the three rooms.
That is the module's answer to "and when not to" in its own job line.

Twenty-three keys bought, under `newWordCap`: `अरे`, `अगं`, `चेष्टा`, `करतेस`, `रे`, `कळतं`, `गं`,
`विसरलीस`, `विसरलास`, `वा`, `केलंस`, `मीच`, `तेच`, `तसंच`, `सहज`, `विनोद`, `हसला`, `हसली`, `बापरे`,
`खोटं`, `चिडवू`, `चिडवतो`, `गंमत`.

## Seams — what `content:owner` said, and where the briefs needed correcting

### 1. The body-word seam is real, and it is exactly as the L5-M1 brief describes it

```
डोकं	L3-M7
हात	L3-M7
डोळे	L3-M7
पाय	L3-M7
पोट	L2-M5
तोंड	free
कान	free
नाक	free
```

Confirmed unchanged at 40 modules. So `तोंड` and `कान` could be bought by this module and `डोकं`
could not, and the module is built around that asymmetry: the two idioms whose noun it owns get
word rows for the noun (S04, S05), and the one whose noun it does not (S01–S03) puts the whole
weight on `rules[0]`, `rules[2]` and the `literal` line. A learner tapping `डोकं` inside
`डोकं खाणे` is shown L3-M7's body-part note, which is true of the word and false of the sentence;
that is a known and accepted consequence of first-occurrence-wins, not a defect this wave can fix,
and it is question 119 below.

### 2. The homograph trap held, and this module has now closed the spelling

```
म्हण	free
म्हणी	free
म्हणतो	free
म्हणाला	L3-M5
म्हणतात	free
```

L5-M1 takes `म्हण` as the **noun** (`forms: ["म्हण", "म्हणी"]`), so the spelling `म्हण` is now
owned product-wide and **no later L5 display may write the bare imperative `म्हण`**. Both files
honour that: nothing in either module writes it, and where an imperative was wanted the module used
`नकोस` / `नको` frames instead. The brief's suggested escape (`सांगा → L2-M4`) was not needed.
`म्हणतो` was deliberately left free — a later module may still want the first-person — while
`म्हणतात`, which the brief did not list, is bought here as the frame verb for a proverb.

### 3. A seam neither brief names: personal names are NOT exempt from `check-shown`

The L5-M2 brief's first pattern is `अरे / अगं + <name> + , + clause`, and says nothing about which
names the index holds. It matters, because `#61`'s proper-noun exemption is a *build* convention;
`tools/check-shown.ts` has no proper-noun list at all and reports a free name as a plain
`SHOWN-BUT-UNTAUGHT`. (`tools/shown-surfaces.test.ts`'s own header confirms the direction: `प्रिया`
is one of the seven hi-mr baseline findings *because* it rides unindexed.)

```
रोहन	L1-M1
राहुल	free
सागर	free
अमित	free
सीमा	free
प्रिया	free
नेहा	free
स्नेहा	free
आई	L2-M2
आजी	L4-M8
```

So the vocative sentences had to be built from surfaces the index already owns: `अरे रोहन` (S01)
and `अगं आई` (S02), with `अगं आजी` as a variation. Of every name checked, `रोहन` is the only one the index owns and there is no
indexed female name at all, so the feminine vocative can only be demonstrated on a kinship term. That is a real hole in the course and it is question 127.

### 4. `दिसतं → free` while `तसं → L3-M3` and `नसतं → L4-M3` — a three-word proverb for one key

```
दिसतं	free
तसं	L3-M3
नसतं	L4-M3
```

`दिसतं तसं नसतं` is a complete म्हण that costs this module a single key, because L3-M3 and L4-M3
already bought the other two words for entirely unrelated reasons (comparison, and the
counterfactual). That is what made a *second* proverb affordable inside a 25-key cap, and it is the
reason the module can show that a म्हण is a category and not one memorised string. The brief listed
neither word.

### 5. The `-च्या` fusion, checked cell by cell before any forms list was written

```
देवाच्या	free
देवाची	free
कृपेने	free
कृपा	free
नशिबाने	free
नशीब	free
नशिबात	free
```

The brief's rule — "bare `कृपा` and bare `नशीब` get keys only if a display writes them standing
apart" — was applied literally. `नशीब` **is** written standing apart (S10 variation 1,
`माझं नशीब चांगलं असतं तर किती बरं झालं असतं`), so it is bought, as the second entry of
`नशिबाने`'s forms. `कृपा` is **never** written apart, so it stays free and only `कृपेने` is bought.
`देवाची` is bought as the second cell of `देवाच्या`'s row because S10 writes it
(`देवाची इच्छा असती तर …`), and S09's `mistake` is precisely the crossed pair,
`देवाची कृपेने`. `नशिबात` was checked and left free.

### 6. The clitic seam is exactly as the L5-M2 brief states it

```
च	free
आत्ताच	L1-M3
मीच	free
तेच	free
तसंच	free
```

Confirmed verbatim. The precedent the brief points at — a welded-`च` word as its own single-token
key, `आत्ताच` from L1-M3 — is real, and this module follows it: three separate rows, one per welded
form, each pointing back at its bare host in the note, and **no row for bare `च`**, which no display
writes. Rule 1 names `आत्ताच` explicitly so the learner sees that the shape is old and only the use
is new.

### 7. The L5-M2 brief's "surprise worth checking" checks out, and cost nothing

```
राग	L1-M9
मजा	L1-M9
छान	L1-M9
खरं	L3-M3
खरंच	L4-M4
काय	L1-M2
बरं	L1-M2
ना	L3-M3
तू	L1-M2
पण	L4-M2
```

All ten confirmed at their claimed owners. The brief flags `राग → L1-M9` and `मजा → L1-M9` as worth
re-checking before writing a row for either; both hold, and this module writes a row for **neither**
— `राग` and `मजा` appear nowhere in either file's displays. `खोटं` is bought instead, as `खरं`'s
opposite, and its note points at L3-M3 rather than re-opening it. The result is that **this wave
reports zero `RE-TEACH` on either module**: every row in both files opens a key no earlier module
owns.

### 8. Three synonyms were on offer and only two were taken

```
चेष्टा	free
मस्करी	free
थट्टा	free
गंमत	free
विनोद	free
```

The brief lists five fresh keys for one semantic field. `चेष्टा` (teasing aimed at someone),
`विनोद` (a joke as an object) and `गंमत` (fun, and the stop line) are bought because each does a
different job in the module; `मस्करी` and `थट्टा` are left free. `मस्करी` survives only inside
S10's `mistake.why`, which the index never reads. Spending three of twenty-five keys on three ways
to say the same thing would have bought the learner nothing, and leaves L5-M3 (regional style) the
room to take one if a variety needs it.

### 9. `सहज` and `चेष्टा` are false friends, and both are tagged `interference`

Neither brief says so, and both are worth the reader's attention: Marathi `चेष्टा` is a *joke*
where Hindi `चेष्टा` is an *effort*, and Marathi `सहज` is *in passing* where Hindi `सहज` is *easy*.
A Hindi speaker reads both wrong without noticing, because both misreadings produce a sentence that
still parses. They are the `trap` on S02 and S08 respectively, and rule 4's closing clause. The same
class caught L5-M1's `सारखं`, which is *continually* in Marathi and *like* in Hindi — also tagged
`interference`, also a `trap`.

## The ratchet

`tools/shown-surfaces.test.ts` holds hi-mr at **7**, and this wave leaves it at 7. Both new modules
are clean:

- `npm run content:shown -- hi-mr L5-M1` → `L5-M1: clean — every shown surface resolves`
- `npm run content:shown -- hi-mr L5-M2` → `L5-M2: clean — every shown surface resolves`

No `SHOWN-BUT-UNTAUGHT`, no `COLLIDES INSIDE THIS MODULE`, and **no `RE-TEACH` reported on either
module** — the tail that would have said "n re-teach(es) reported above" is absent from both lines
because there were none. All twenty hero displays, all forty variation displays and all
twenty-four comprehension items resolve in the cumulative index, including the two-clause displays
(L5-M1-S06, S07, S08; L5-M2-S07, S10), which resolve on both halves.

The baseline was not lowered, and could not have been: all seven existing findings are in hi-mr L1
(`थोडं`, `भाजी`, `पाच`, `झाले` taught later; `प्रिया`, `पुणं`, `बोललो` never taught — docs/52), and
a level never edits a file below it. Lowering them is L1's sweep, not this wave's. Seam 3 above adds
a reason to want that sweep sooner: `प्रिया` sitting unindexed is why L5-M2 could not put a woman's
name after `अगं`.

`npm run content:validate` → `CONTENT 378/378 ok` (the total climbs while the nine sibling waves
land). `npx vitest run tools/shown-surfaces.test.ts` → 11 passed. `npx vitest run
src/course/types.test.ts` → 397 passed, 2 failed, and both failures are file-census counts moved by
sibling waves — the module list assertion (the parent's) and the en-ar module count. Every course
law in that file passes for both new modules, including the enrichment rule that M1–M3 carry
`sound`, `variations`, `mistake`, `usage` and `mnemonic` on **every** sentence: both files do, on
all twenty.

## Open questions for a native pass — hi-mr L5 wave 1

Continuing the chain in `docs/103-hi-mr-L5-brief-decisions.md`, whose last number is 118. Nothing
above is renumbered. The native-speaker gate is a stricter bar than the LLM review that shipped
these two modules, and it is unmet; none of these may be closed by rewriting a shipped module.

119. **`डोकं खाणे` and the note a learner is actually shown.** First occurrence wins, so tapping
     `डोकं` inside `त्याने माझं डोकं खाल्लं` shows L3-M7's literal body-part note. Rule 2 and every
     `literal` line are built to compensate. Is that enough in practice, or does the pairing of a
     literal note with a figurative sentence actively mislead — and if it does, is the remedy an
     app-side one (a "this word is part of an idiom" marker) rather than a content one?
120. **`तोंड देणे` against `सामना करणे`.** S04's `mistake` asserts that
     `मला संकटाचा सामना करावा लागतो` is a Hindi sentence in Marathi clothes and that a Pune speaker
     says `संकटाला तोंड देतो`. Confirm that `सामना करणे` is genuinely not said — it appears in
     written and news Marathi — and if it *is* said in some registers, say which, because then the
     `mistake` is too absolute.
121. **`कान भरणे` as the clean calque.** S05 is built on the claim that Hindi `कान भरना` and Marathi
     `कान भरणे` are the same idiom with the same force. Confirm the force is the same — is the
     Marathi one milder, or more accusatory? And is `माझे कान भरू नकोस` the shape a speaker uses,
     or is the third party normally named (`त्याचे कान भरू नकोस`)?
122. **The two म्हणी chosen.** `अति तिथे माती` and `दिसतं तसं नसतं`. Are these the two a learner
     meets first, or are they the two an outsider has read about? Name the five most-heard म्हणी in
     ordinary Pune speech, and say whether either of these is in that five.
123. **The frame for quoting a म्हण.** S07 puts `म्हणतात ना` after the proverb and S08 puts
     `असं म्हणतात` after it too; the brief's pattern has `म्हणतात ना` postposed. Is one order the
     ordinary one and the other bookish? Is bare `… म्हणतात ना` ever fronted?
124. **`देवाच्या कृपेने` and register.** S09 presents it as an everyday way to credit an outcome.
     Does it read as genuinely neutral, or as devout — and would a speaker who is not religious use
     it, the way English "thank God" survives unbelief?
125. **The if-only frame's shape.** S10 ships `देवाची इच्छा असती तर किती बरं झालं असतं`. Confirm
     `असती` with `इच्छा`, and confirm that `किती बरं झालं असतं` is the everyday tail rather than a
     written one. Is there a shorter spoken form (`बरं झालं असतं`) that is what people actually say?
126. **`अरे` to a woman, one level on from question 114.** Q114 asks how it reads. This wave has now
     committed: it is the `mistake` on two of ten sentences, and rule 4 calls it "instantly
     foreign". If a native pass finds it merely rural or generational rather than foreign, both
     `mistake.why` lines are wrong in the same way and the module's central error needs replacing.
127. **One name, and no woman's name.** `रोहन → L1-M1` is the only personal name the hi-mr index
     owns; `प्रिया`, `सीमा`, `नेहा` and the rest are all free, and `check-shown` does not exempt
     proper nouns. So `अगं` could only be demonstrated on `आई` / `आजी`. Is a kinship term an
     adequate stand-in for a friend's name in this register, or does the vocative land differently
     when the addressee is family? Either way this points at an L1 fix, not an L5 one.
128. **`वा, छान … केलंस!` without a voice.** Rule 3 and S05's `trap` both say the ironic reading is
     carried by tone, which a silent, audio-free product cannot supply. Is the frame still readable
     as irony on the page — does `वा` plus an obviously false claim do the work — or is a
     text-only learner being taught a form they cannot actually deploy?
129. **`बापरे` as one written word.** S09 asserts Marathi writes it solid where Hindi writes
     `बाप रे` in two. Confirm the spelling convention, and say whether `बाप रे` spaced is simply an
     error or an accepted variant.
130. **`ही गंमत नाही` as a fixed stop line.** S10 and rule 5 treat it as *the* sentence that ends
     teasing. Is it that conventionalised, or is it one option among several — and what does a
     speaker say when the teasing has to stop but the friendship must not?

## Wave 2 — L5-M3, L5-M4 and L5-M5 (#572)

### L5-M3 "How they say it there" — the module that admits the course taught one Marathi

Ten displays:

1. `आपण पुणेरी मराठी शिकतो, पण बोली खूप आहेत.`
2. `कोकणात लोक थोडं वेगळं बोलतात, पण सगळं समजतं.`
3. `तू काय म्हणतोस, हे बरोबर आहे का?`
4. `तुम्ही काय म्हणताय, हे बरोबर आहे का?`
5. `आजोबा काय म्हणतात, ते नीट ऐक.`
6. `आजकाल तरुण इंग्रजी शब्द वापरतात, पूर्वी असं नव्हतं.`
7. `हल्ली तरुण पिढी वेगळी मराठी बोलते.`
8. `इकडे आम्ही असं म्हणतो, तिकडे तसं म्हणतात.`
9. `मराठीत जास्त म्हणतात, ज्यादा हा हिंदी शब्द आहे.`
10. `गावाकडे उच्चार वेगळा असतो, पण शब्द तेच असतात.`

What it teaches, in twenty-five keys and five module rules. S01 is the module's whole reason for
existing and it says the thing out loud: `आपण पुणेरी मराठी शिकतो` — the course has been teaching ONE
Marathi and here is its name. The three axes the brief's note 2 fixes are each one minimal pair and
no more. **Region** is S02 and S10: `कोकणात` and `गावाकडे` against the Pune standard, with the
coastal `हाय`/`नाय` carried as comprehension only. **Honorific** is S03, S04 and S05 — the same
sentence three times with one cell changed, which is the axis note 2 says the learner is actually
judged on: `तू … म्हणतोस` / `तुम्ही … म्हणताय` / `आजोबा … म्हणतात`. **Generation** is S06 and S07:
`आजकाल` and `हल्ली` against L4-M8's `पूर्वी`, and the young speaker's `इंग्रजी शब्द वापरतात` against a
grandparent who does not.

Rule 3 is the module's most useful sentence and it is the one note 4 demanded: the learner's own
markers — `ज्यादा` for `जास्त`, `लेकिन` for `पण`, `बहुत` for `खूप`, L4-M7's Hindi-shaped `केला जातो`
where the sign says `करण्यात येईल` — are **not a Marathi dialect**. They read as Hindi-accented
Marathi, which is a different and worse thing, and S09 makes that a display. The same rule says
Hindi's middle tier `तुम` has no Marathi twin at all; S03's `mistake` is exactly that, a learner
writing `तुम काय म्हणतोस`, and the `why` says the word does not exist rather than that it is rude.

Rule 4 carries note 5's ban: no variety here is *better*, and what marks an outsider is mixing them
rather than choosing one. Nothing in the module ranks a variety, and the `usage` lines describe
rather than correct.

**How `हाय` and `नाय` are shipped without ever being produced.** Note 3 is absolute — everything the
learner PRODUCES stays Pune standard, and the variety spelling lives in `usage` and in the
comprehension pool. That is a mechanical problem, because *a shown surface is a taught surface*: a
pool item is scanned and must resolve. The resolution is that `tools/check-shown.ts` (and the
emitter it imports from) scans `display`, variation displays and pool displays, while a word ROW is
free to teach a surface the sentence does not contain. So `हाय` and `नाय` are two rows on S02 —
the Konkan sentence — whose own display is Pune standard throughout; the varieties appear in S02's
`usage` (`तो घरी आहे` against `तो घरी हाय`) and as pool items C03 and C04, and in no display anywhere.
The rows carry the route back (`आहे (L1-M1)`, `नाही (L1-M2)`) and say in as many words: recognise,
never write. Both are tagged `delta`, not `free`, because being able to hear them is the delta.

**Paradigm holes.** The honorific spread is a three-cell paradigm and this module owns only two
cells: `म्हणतोस` (S03) and `म्हणताय` (S04) each list nothing but themselves in `forms`. The third
cell, `म्हणतात`, is L5-M1's — see the seam section — so it appears in S05, S08 and S09 as a re-shown
surface with **no row at all**, and rule 1 says whose it is. A `forms` list on either row that
reached for `म्हणतात` would have been a collision against a shipped module, which is the en-it
`sarei` lesson one course over. `म्हणतो` gets its own row on S08 (the first-person cell, free)
and its note prints the whole ladder so the hole is visible rather than silent.

`ऐक` (S05) is the other shape of an older lexeme: `ऐकतो` is L1-M4's and `ऐकलं` is L1-M5's, so the
imperative gets its own row in the module that first shows it, with the note pointing back at L1-M4
— never an edit to a file below. Its `forms` carries `ऐका` too, the तुम्ही imperative, because S05's
variation shows it.

### L5-M4 "Formal occasions" — where the learner builds a passive for the first time

Ten displays:

1. `उद्या शाळेत एक मोठा कार्यक्रम आयोजित करण्यात आला आहे.`
2. `हा समारंभ मोठा आहे, इथे तुमचे स्वागत आहे.`
3. `आज इथे खूप लोक उपस्थित आहेत.`
4. `मी सर्व उपस्थित मान्यवरांचे आभार मानतो.`
5. `तुमचे अभिनंदन! हे खूप मोठे यश आहे.`
6. `तुम्हाला वाढदिवसाच्या खूप खूप शुभेच्छा!`
7. `तुम्हाला लग्नाच्या खूप खूप शुभेच्छा!`
8. `तुम्हाला मोठे आयुष्य आणि खूप यश मिळो.`
9. `त्यांचे निधन झाले, आणि त्यांना श्रद्धांजली वाहण्यात आली.`
10. `ईश्वर त्यांच्या आत्म्यास शांती देवो.`

The three occasions note 1 names get a block each: the event and the welcome (S01–S03), the
public thank-you (S04) and the congratulation (S05–S08), the death (S09–S10). Every one is a set
piece with fixed wording, which rule 4 says out loud, because note 1's claim is that a learner who
improvises these sounds worse than one who has three memorised lines.

**The productive passive** is rule 0 and it is the deferral `docs/48` §4 amendment 2 opened.
L4-M7 taught `करण्यात येतं / येईल / आलं` as something the learner READS and owns both `करण्यात` and
`देण्यात`; this module does not write a second row for either — they are re-shown in S01 and S02 and
the productive step lives in `rules[0]`, exactly as the brief's note 5 instructed. What IS new is
`वाहण्यात` (S09), its own single-token key with a note pointing at the base verb `वाहणे`, exactly as
L4-M7's `-ण्यात` forms are; bare `ण्यात` stands free nowhere. Three sentences show the frame
completing on `आला` (S01), `आले` (S02's variation) and `आली` (S09), so the agreement rule that
`rules[0]` states — the `-ण्यात` word never changes, `येणे` carries the tense, `आला/आली/आलं` carries
the agreement — is visible three times rather than asserted once.

**The ceremonial optative** is rule 1: `मिळो` (S08) and `देवो` (S10), the `-ओ` that blesses. Rule 1
draws the line note 2 demanded against L4-M7's notice optative `-आवे` (`कृपया रांगेत उभे राहावे`) in
one sentence: `-ओ` wishes, `-आवे` tells. S10's `mistake` is the sharpest test of it — a learner who
writes `ईश्वर … शांती मिळो` has produced a sentence in which nobody is doing the giving and `ईश्वर`
sits idle, which is a grammatical fact rather than a taste.

**Where the L1 helps, and the module says so.** Rule 2 is the honest one note 3 asked for: the
occasion vocabulary is Sanskrit and carries over nearly outright — `अभिनंदन`, `श्रद्धांजली`, `स्वागत`,
`आभार`, `समारंभ`, `उपस्थित`, `ईश्वर`, `शांती` — and no delta is manufactured for them; they are tagged
`free`. The interference is placed exactly where note 3 puts it, on the VERB and on one greeting:
`श्रद्धांजली वाहणे` against Hindi `अर्पित करना` (S09's `mistake`), `आभार मानणे` against Hindi
`आभार व्यक्त करना` (S04's `mistake`), `शुभेच्छा` against Hindi `शुभकामनाएँ` (S06's `mistake`), and
`अभिनंदन` taking no `हो` (S05's `mistake`). Four of the ten `mistake` blocks are the same failure
mode — reach for the shared noun, then attach the Hindi verb — which is what note 3 predicted.

`कृपया`, `धन्यवाद` and `नमस्कार` are L2-M1's and L1-M2's and are re-shown (S04's first variation ends
on `धन्यवाद`), never re-opened. Nothing from L3-M8's bank window appears: rule 4 says a condolence is
not an announcement.

### L5-M5 "Big questions" — the first module whose subject is not in the room

Ten displays:

1. `माझ्या मते प्रेम आणि माणुसकी हा सगळ्यात मोठा धर्म आहे.`
2. `खोटं बोलणं चूक आहे, पण खरं बोलणं अवघड असतं.`
3. `माणसाने नेहमी खरं बोललं पाहिजे.`
4. `प्रामाणिकपणा हा मोठा गुण आहे, पण तो अवघड आहे.`
5. `चांगुलपणा मोठा आहे, पण मोठेपणा चांगला नाही.`
6. `समाजात समानता पाहिजे, कारण माणूस म्हणून सगळे समान आहेत.`
7. `माझ्या मते स्वातंत्र्य आणि न्याय हा एक विचार आहे.`
8. `सत्य सांगणं अवघड असतं, पण ते गरजेचं आहे.`
9. `जग मोठं आहे, पण जीवन लहान आहे.`
10. `माझ्या मते सुख वेगळं आहे आणि समाधान वेगळं आहे.`

The system is NOMINALISATION and it is three devices, one rule each. **Device 1**, `-पणा` on an
adjective (rule 0): `चांगुलपणा`, `मोठेपणा`, `प्रामाणिकपणा`, all three shipped, all three as their own
single-token keys with notes pointing at the adjective they are built from. The rule says the stem
shifts — `चांगला` → `चांगुल-`, `मोठा` → `मोठे-` — so each derived word is learned rather than computed,
and S05's `mistake` is a learner writing `चांगलापणा`. The bare suffix gets no key, exactly as bare
`मुळे` and bare `पेक्षा` do not. **Device 2**, `-ता` on a Sanskrit stem (rule 1): `समानता` in S06,
with rule 1 saying which adjectives can take it and S06's `mistake` (`समानपणा`) showing the wrong
device on the right word. **Device 3**, the `-णं` verbal noun as SUBJECT (rule 2): `बोलणं` (S02) and
`सांगणं` (S08), the shape the learner will use most and the one that costs nothing new, since `-णे`
has been the course's citation form since L1. What is new is the POSITION, and both `mistake`s test
it from opposite sides: S08's is `सांगणे` left in citation form where the subject slot needs `सांगणं`,
S02's is `आहेत` where the neuter singular `-णं` subject requires `आहे`.

The generic obligative is rule 3 and S03: `माणसाने नेहमी खरं बोललं पाहिजे`, built on L3-M4's `पाहिजे`
with nothing new but the ergative-marked `माणसाने`. Its `mistake` is `बोलणे पाहिजे`, the direct
Hindi calque of `बोलना चाहिए`, which the `trap` names as the module's biggest single slip: Marathi
puts a PAST form where Hindi puts the infinitive, though the statement is about the future.

Rule 4 is the module's interference rule and it is the one note 3 called the most productive Hindi
habit failing: Hindi's `-ई` — `अच्छाई`, `सच्चाई`, `बुराई` — does not exist in Marathi. S04's `mistake`
is `प्रामाणिकताई`, a word the learner invents with total confidence because the suffix works
everywhere in the L1.

Rule 5 records what the seam found: `माझ्या मते` costs nothing, because `माझ्या` is L2-M2's and `मते`
is L3-M3's and this course runs at `maxSpan` 1, so the frame is two owned words standing together
and has no key of its own. Three displays open on it (S01, S07, S10) and no row was written for
either half.

The module stops where note 4 says it must. Nothing here answers back an objection — that is
L5-M6 — and nothing is a proverb (L5-M1's) or a ceremony (L5-M4's).

### The seams the briefs got wrong, and what `content:owner` actually said

The briefs' INDEX SEAM sections were written against an older fold. The index today reports
**857 surfaces owned, folded over 42 modules through L5-M2**, and the brief's own framing —
"N surfaces through L3-M10" in the L4 briefs, and `docs/103`'s L5 plan — is two rungs behind it.
Four claims failed, and all four failed in the same direction: a key the brief called `free` had
been taken by a module authored since.

131. **`म्हणतात` is `L5-M1`, not free.** The L5-M3 brief's note 6 hedged this correctly — "`म्हणतात`
   is this module's if it ships first; L5-M8's `याला काय म्हणतात?` and L5-M9's `असं म्हणतात` both
   re-show it and point back, and the authoring order decides" — and the authoring order decided
   against it. `npm run content:owner -- hi-mr म्हणतात` prints `म्हणतात	L5-M1`; the shipped module
   bought it inside `असं म्हणतात`. So L5-M3 writes **no row** for it, re-shows it in three displays
   (S05, S08, S09), and `rules[1]` names L5-M1 as its owner while carrying the third cell of the
   honorific paradigm. The hedge is what saved the module; a brief that had simply written
   `म्हणतात → free` would have produced an unreachable row.

132. **`नशीब` is `L5-M1`, not free.** `docs/103`'s L5-M5 note 5 lists `नशीब → free` among that
   module's fresh keys. It is not: `npm run content:owner -- hi-mr नशीब` prints `नशीब	L5-M1`, and
   `नशिबाने` and `देवाच्या` sit beside it in the same shipped module — L5-M1 is the proverbs module
   and luck is proverb vocabulary. L5-M5 ships no `नशीब` row and no `नशीब` display; the abstract
   ground it would have covered is carried by `धर्म` (S01) and `जग` (S09) instead.

133. **`खोटं` is `L5-M2`, not free.** The L5-M5 brief's note 2 gives `खोटं बोलणं चूक आहे` as the
   worked example of device 3 — and `npm run content:owner -- hi-mr खोटं` prints `खोटं	L5-M2`,
   which bought it for the teasing register. The display survives verbatim as S02, because a
   re-shown owned surface needs no row; only the row that the brief implied had to go. The single
   new key in S02 is `बोलणं`. Nothing was lost and the sentence the brief wanted is on the page.

134. **`अर्थ` is free today and this module leaves it free.** `docs/103`'s L5-M5 note 5 assigns
   `अर्थ` to L5-M8 (`याचा अर्थ काय?`) and says L5-M5 re-shows it. L5-M8 does not exist yet, so a
   re-show would have been `SHOWN-BUT-UNTAUGHT`. L5-M5 therefore uses `अर्थ` in no display and in
   no row, and the assignment stands untouched for whoever authors L5-M8.

Two brief claims that LOOKED wrong and were right, checked rather than assumed:

135. **`इकडे → free`, `तिकडे → free`, against `इथे → L2-M4` and `तिथे → L2-M4`.** The L5-M3 brief's
   note 6 flags this as the place the first instinct goes wrong, and `content:owner` confirms it
   exactly: `इकडे	free`, `तिकडे	free`, `इथे	L2-M4`, `तिथे	L2-M4`. They are separate

   `इथे` is a spot, `इकडे` is a direction or a region — so a learner tapping `इकडे` gets a note about

136. **`करण्यात → L4-M7` and `देण्यात → L4-M7`.** True, and the L5-M4 brief's instruction follows from
   it: those rows exist and their notes are announcement notes, so this module re-shows them and

Two smaller corrections that are the authors' rather than the briefs':

137. **`वाढदिवस` and `लग्न` stay free; L5-M4 owns only `वाढदिवसाच्या` and `लग्नाच्या`.** The greeting

138. **`म्हातारा` was on the L5-M3 brief's fresh-key list and is not shipped.** The generational

### Open questions for the native-speaker gate

Continuing the chain: `docs/103` ends at 118 and this file had no numbered questions before this
section, so these begin at 119.

249. **`हाय` and `नाय` as the whole of the regional axis.** L5-M3 ships one coastal minimal pair,
     comprehension-only, on two rows attached to S02 whose display contains neither. `docs/103`'s
     Q115 already asks whether these are the right pair; this wave adds the narrower question. Is
     one pair enough to make the point that a variety exists, or does a learner who meets exactly
     two variant spellings conclude that coastal Marathi differs from Pune Marathi in exactly two
     words? And is `हाय`/`नाय` the pair a Konkan speaker would themselves name first, or the pair a
     Pune speaker notices first?

250. **A word row that teaches a surface the sentence does not contain.** The mechanism above is
     legal — the build indexes rows, and only displays, variation displays and pool items are
     scanned — but it has not been used deliberately anywhere else in the nine courses. On the
     Sentence Detail screen, S02's deconstruction lists five rows of which two are words the
     learner cannot find in the line above. Does that read as a dialect footnote, which is what it
     is, or as a bug? If it reads as a bug, the alternative is a display that produces a variety
     the module has just forbidden the learner to produce, and note 3 forbids that outright.

251. **`म्हणताय` as the तुम्ही cell.** L5-M3-S04 teaches the contracted `म्हणताय` rather than the
     full `म्हणता आहात`, on the claim that the contraction is what a Pune speaker actually says and
     that the full form would make the honorific axis look bookish beside `म्हणतोस`. Confirm the
     contraction is neutral rather than casual, since S04 is registered `formal` and its `usage`
     sends the learner to use it with elders and at work. If it is casual, the minimal pair breaks:
     the तू cell would be plain and the तुम्ही cell marked.

252. **`ज्यादा` shipped as a display.** L5-M3-S09 puts the Hindi word ON THE PAGE — `ज्यादा हा हिंदी
     शब्द आहे` — as its own `interference` row, so that the index has an entry for the thing the
     learner is doing wrong. That is the first time this course names an L1 word as a display rather
     than in a `trap` or a `mistake`. Does showing it teach it? The alternative is to describe it in
     `rules[3]` only, where a learner never taps it.

253. **`आभार मानतो` and the reflex it must beat.** L5-M4-S04's `mistake` is `आभार व्यक्त करतो`, on
     the claim that Hindi `आभार व्यक्त करना` calqued into Marathi is wrong rather than merely
     stiff. Is `व्यक्त करणे` genuinely unavailable with `आभार`, or is it heard from the stage in
     Pune and only preferred against? The rule is stated absolutely ("the pair is `आभार` + `मानणे`
     and it does not break") and an absolute rule that is really a preference is the memorable and
     false kind note 5 of the L5-M3 brief warns about.

254. **`श्रद्धांजली वाहणे` against `श्रद्धांजली अर्पण करणे`.** Same shape, higher stakes, and the
     brief names only the Hindi `अर्पित करना` as the trap. `अर्पण` is free in the index and is a
     Marathi word. Confirm that `वाहणे` is the condolence verb in written and spoken Marathi both,
     and that `अर्पण करणे` is not simply the more formal of two live options — S09 is the sentence
     of this module that a learner is most likely to send to a grieving family.

255. **`ईश्वर` against `देव` in a condolence.** L5-M4-S10 uses `ईश्वर` and its row says the
     condolence formula takes `ईश्वर` rather than `देव`, on the reasoning that it is the register-
     neutral, cross-community word. L5-M1 already owns `देवाची` and `देवाच्या` for the proverbs, so
     the two coexist in the index. Confirm the split is real and that `देव त्यांच्या आत्म्यास शांती
     देवो` is not simply the commoner spoken form.

256. **The `-ओ` optative's productivity.** L5-M4 ships exactly two, `मिळो` and `देवो`, and
     `rules[1]` presents `-ओ` as a mood a learner can recognise everywhere and produce in fixed
     phrases. Is it productive enough that `यश मिळो` licenses, say, `सुख लाभो`? If it is, the module
     is under-teaching a live pattern; if it is not, the rule should say the two are memorised items
     and the ending is not a tool. The brief's phrasing ("the ceremonial optative in `-ओ`") does not
     decide it.

257. **`चांगुलपणा`'s stem.** L5-M5-S05 teaches `चांगुल-` as an irregular shift from `चांगला` and
     makes `चांगलापणा` the `mistake`. Confirm the shift and, more importantly, confirm the ceiling:
     `rules[0]` tells the learner that each `-पणा` word is learned rather than computed, which is
     safe advice but may be too conservative if the suffix is genuinely productive on
     ordinary adjectives.

258. **`समाधान` as the module's silent interference.** L5-M5-S10 makes Hindi `समाधान` = *solution*
     against Marathi `समाधान` = *contentment* the sentence's `trap`, on the same shape as L5-M2's
     `चेष्टा`. Confirm that the Marathi word does not ALSO carry *solution* in ordinary use — if it
     does, the trap is a half-truth and the pairing with `सुख` is the only thing keeping S10 honest.

259. **`जग` as the everyday word for *world*.** S09's `trap` claims the register is inverted against
     Hindi — `जग` bookish there, ordinary here. Confirm, and confirm the neuter gender the `mistake`
     turns on (`जग मोठं`, not `जग मोठा`). If `जग` is in fact bookish in Marathi too, S09's honest
     word is `जगात` or a different sentence, and the trap dissolves.

260. **Whether `माझ्या मते` opening three of ten displays is one too many.** It is free — two owned
     tokens, no key — and that is exactly the risk: a frame that costs nothing gets over-used. A
     native reader should say whether a Marathi speaker actually prefaces beliefs this often, or
     whether the ordinary way in is `मला वाटतं` (L3-M3) and `माझ्या मते` is the written one.

## Seams — what `content:owner` said, and where the wave 3 briefs needed correcting

Five of the fourteen INDEX SEAM claims across these five briefs were wrong or stale, and all five
were wrong in the same direction: a surface the brief calls free has since been bought by an
EARLIER MODULE OF THIS SAME LEVEL. Waves 1 and 2 shipped between the briefs being written and this

### 10. `म्हणजे → L5-M1`, not free — and it is the L5-M6 brief's headline claim

The L5-M6 brief's note 6 opens: *"AGAINST INSTINCT, twice, and both were checked rather than carried
over: मात्र → free and म्हणजे → free."* Today:

```
म्हणजे	L5-M1
मात्र	free
```

Half of it held. `मात्र` is free and L5-M6 bought it. `म्हणजे` was bought by wave 1's L5-M1-S02,
`डोकं खाणे म्हणजे सारखं त्रास देणे` — the sayings module needed *"X म्हणजे Y"* to gloss a proverb, and

for it; a learner tapping it in `पहिलं म्हणजे …` is shown L5-M1's proverb-glossing note, which is

## Open questions for a native pass — hi-mr L5 wave 3

Continuing this course's chain. The last number used is **260**, at the end of the wave 2 section
above; wave 2 also spent 131-138 on its seam list, and `docs/103-hi-mr-L5-brief-decisions.md` runs
to 118. Nothing above is renumbered. The native-speaker
gate is a stricter bar than the LLM review that shipped these five modules, and it is unmet; none of
these may be closed by rewriting a shipped module.

261. **`म्हणजे` teaches the wrong note under L5-M6.** Seam 10: L5-M1 owns the key, so a learner
     tapping `म्हणजे` inside `पहिलं म्हणजे मराठी शिकणं सोपं नाही` is shown L5-M1's proverb-glossing
     note, not the enumerating one. L5-M6's rule 0 carries the enumerating fact instead. Is that
     enough, or is `पहिलं म्हणजे … दुसरं म्हणजे …` different enough from `X म्हणजे Y` that a
     Marathi speaker would want it named on the word?

262. **`मात्र` against `पण`, and whether `परंतु` deserves a mention at all.** L5-M6's rule 1 puts
     `मात्र` exactly one step above `पण` and rule 6 rules `परंतु` out as bookish. S04's `mistake`
     is `… परंतु मराठी वेगळी आहे.` Is `परंतु` really out of reach in spoken argument, or does an
     educated Marathi speaker use it often enough that calling it a mistake overshoots?

263. **`अर्थात` tagged `interference`.** The module treats Marathi `अर्थात` as *beshak* and Hindi
     `अर्थात्` as *yānī*, and builds S09 on the conceding sense. Does spoken Marathi also use
     `अर्थात` in the explaining sense often enough that the `interference` tag is too strong?

264. **The self-announcing jibe.** L5-M7-S10 is `वा, छान केलंस! हा टोमणा आहे, कौतुक नाही.` — the
     sentence says it is sarcastic because the product has no audio (rule 5). No real speaker
     annotates their own jibe. Is there a display that carries the irony on the situation alone,
     inside thirteen words, that a reader without intonation could not mistake for praise?

265. **`टोमणा` against `उपरोध`.** The brief lists both free. L5-M7 buys `टोमणा` (the concrete jibe
     you throw) and leaves `उपरोध` (the rhetorical device) unbought, on the argument that a learner
     needs the thing before the name for it. Is `टोमणा` the word a Marathi speaker actually uses in
     the moment, or is it more written than spoken?

266. **`होतंय` as the written form.** L5-M7-S05 ships `खूप गरम होतंय ना?` and rule 2 calls `होतंय`
     the spoken contraction of `होत आहे`. Is `होतंय` written this way, or is the display better as
     `होत आहे` with the contraction named only in `sound`?

267. **Three rows for one relative pronoun.** L5-M8 gives `जो`, `जी` and `जे` separate rows so each
     sentence can open one, rather than a single row whose `forms` carry the paradigm — the reason
     is mechanical (a paradigm row makes the later rows unreachable) and not pedagogical. Does the
     split read as three words to a learner where it should read as one word with three genders?

268. **Ten re-teaches at L5-M10.** The module spends nothing, so every row shows an earlier note and
     what it adds about register lives in `rules[]`. A learner tapping `भर` is told L3-M8's fact
     about filling in a form, not that `भर` is the `तू` tier of S02's `भरा`. Is the rule enough at
     the very last module, where there is no later module to repair it?

269. **The switch scene itself.** L5-M10 turns a bank clerk into a friend at S05 and drops to `तू`.
     Is that a switch a Marathi speaker would actually make at a counter with a queue behind them,
     or would they hold `तुम्ही` until the business was finished and switch only on the way out?

270. **`कथा` against `गोष्ट` at L5-M9.** S08 ships `असं म्हणतात की ती कथा खरी आहे` and the row calls
     `कथा` the bookish, folk-tale word against L3-M3's everyday `गोष्ट`. In a retelling of a film or
     of what happened at the office — which is what the module is for — is `कथा` ever the word, or
     should the module have stayed on `गोष्ट` throughout and spent the row elsewhere?

271. **The honorific retelling.** L5-M9's rule 4 says that when a story is about someone respected,
     the honorific plural runs through the WHOLE retelling (`ते आले, त्यांनी सांगितलं`), not just
     the sentence that mentions them. Is that right, and does it hold when the respected person and
     an ordinary character appear in the same sentence?

272. **`साहेब` is still unspent at the end of the ladder.** `npm run content:owner -- hi-mr साहेब`
     returns `free` after fifty modules. L4-M7's brief claimed it and did not buy it; L5-M10 was
     told not to. Should a fifty-module course close with no word for addressing a stranger of
     rank, or is that a real gap the L1 sweep or a future level should fill?
