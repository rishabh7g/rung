# en-de L3 — the authoring-brief decisions (#468)

The ten en-de L3 briefs (`tools/course-briefs.ts`, `COURSE_BRIEFS['en-de']` L3-M1…L3-M10) are the
eighth L3 briefed in this repo, after hi-mr's (#452, `docs/50`) and en-es's (#462, `docs/69`). Every
seam below was pinned against the REAL cumulative index — the fold of
`public/content/en-de/index/L1-M1.json` through `L2-M10.json`, read on 2026-09-08: **467 surfaces,
maxSpan 3** (L1 closed at 203, so L2 added 264) — and against the review chain the level inherits:
`docs/46` (spoken German), `docs/59`'s L2 decisions, and `docs/67`'s forty open questions.

This note records the decisions the briefs are written to, so the authoring waves inherit them
without re-deriving anything. The briefs repeat each decision in the module notes, because a prompt
only ever shows an author the notes.

## 1. Register — carried from L2, and it is the level's closing device

`docs/59` §1 settled it, and L3 adds no register rule; it applies the one it has.

- L2-M1 paid for `du` in one wave — a second set of endings, its own imperative, `dein` / `dich` /
  `dir` — and every module of this level is free to choose an address without spending anything.
- M2 (colleagues), M7 (the surgery) and M8 (offices and counters) speak `Sie` and chip `formal`;
  M1, M3, M4 and M6 speak whichever the listener earns; M9 stays `neutral` where the sentence is
  safe with anyone.
- **M10 closes the level by telling one account twice** — the same story to a friend and to a
  stranger — which is the device hi-mr's L2-M10 invented and en-de has not used yet.
- The capitals stay written wherever German writes one, even though the fold ignores them
  (`docs/59` decision 2). It is the only visible signal of which `sie` a sentence means.

## 2. What L2 withheld, and where it lands

`docs/59` §3, §4 and §5, plus the deferrals written into the shipped L2 rows themselves, name eight
pieces. Each has an owner now, and this is the list an authoring wave checks itself against.

- **The attributive adjective declension → L3-M2.** The level's biggest single debt. L2 kept every
  adjective predicative and said so three times in content (L2-M2 rule 2, L2-M3 rule 2, L2-M9's
  comparative and superlative rows). M2 opens all three declensions on one principle: the
  gender-and-case signal is carried exactly once inside a noun phrase.
- **The declined comparative and superlative → L3-M2**, with it. `das billigere Auto` and
  `die größte Stadt` are the two shapes L2-M9 refused to write and named as L3's.
- **The separable-verb SPLIT → L3-M1.** `docs/59` §4 froze every separable verb behind a modal or
  inside a participle for an index reason that is still true. A day narrated at length cannot be
  told without splitting them.
- **Konjunktiv II beyond L2-M1's frozen `könnten` → L3-M4**, which is where `docs/59` §5 put it in
  advance. Six cells — `wäre`, `hätte`, `könnte`, `sollte`, `müsste`, `würde` + infinitive — and no
  paradigm.
- **Reported speech → L3-M5**, which L2-M7's row names as the reason a message there is passed on
  with the words themselves.
- **The Plusquamperfekt → L3-M5.** L2-M10 named it as L3's; M5 is where it is cheapest, because
  `war`, `waren`, `hatte` and `hatten` are L1-M5's rows and every participle it needs is L1-M5's or
  L2-M10's.
- **The passive → L3-M9.** L2-M10 named it as L3's. A festival described impersonally is where the
  `werden` passive actually lives, and `man` sits beside it as the everyday alternative.
- **Temporal `als` → L3-M5.** L2-M9's `als` row says in as many words that its other job — "when"
  in a past clause — is L3's business.

Four things this level owes nothing to L2 for but must own anyway, because the jobs need them:
**`um … zu` (M1)**, **reflexive verbs (M6)**, **the genitive (M8)** and **relative clauses (M8)**.

Five things stay OUT of L3 entirely and are named in the brief that touches each edge:

- **`damit`** — the purpose clause with a different subject — is L4-M2's, named at M1's edge.
- **The past counterfactual** (`Wenn ich Zeit gehabt hätte, wäre ich gekommen`) is L4-M3's
  ("What might have been", `docs/48` §4), named at M4's edge.
- **Konjunktiv I** (`er sei`, `er habe`) is the newspaper's reported speech, L4-M7's, named at M5's.
- **The past passive, the `von` agent and the `sein`-passive of a state** are L4-M7's, named at M9's.
- **Time clauses with `bevor`, `während`, `seitdem` and `bis`** are L4-M6's, named at M5's and M7's.
  M5 takes exactly ONE of them, `nachdem`, because it is the conjunction that forces the tense the
  module is teaching.

## 3. Where `docs/59`'s plan and the real index disagree

Two places, and both change what a brief may say.

- **`weil`, `dass` and `wenn` are already owned, law included.** `weil` and `dass` are **L1-M9**'s
  and `wenn` is **L1-M10**'s in the fold, and L1 did not merely ship the words: L1-M9's rules state
  that a subordinating conjunction sends the finite verb last while `denn`, `und`, `aber` and `oder`
  leave it second, and L1-M10 restates the whole three-way split with `dann` and `also` as fronting
  adverbs beside it. So **verb-final subordination is not an L3 debt**, and no L3 module may open a
  row for any of the three or present the law as news. What L1 never showed is the subordinate
  clause standing FIRST, with the main verb landing immediately after the comma — and that is what
  **L3-M3** teaches instead.
- **The separable split already exists in L1.** L1-M4-S02 is `Ich stehe um sieben Uhr auf`, and its
  `auf` row already carries both seats — detached prefix here, preposition elsewhere. `docs/59` §4's
  "the split is deferred to L3" is true of L2's own inventory and of nothing else. So **L3-M1's
  lesson is the split for every OTHER separable verb**, and its device is L1-M4's device copied: a
  `rules[]` entry naming the second seat, never a rival row.

## 4. Seams — the collisions this level meets, and their owners

maxSpan stays 3, and every claim below was read out of the fold rather than guessed.

- **Every stranded prefix lands on an owned key.** `ein` is **L1-M1**'s indefinite article, `an` is
  **L2-M4**'s preposition, `mit` is **L2-M4**'s, `auf` is **L1-M4**'s. First-occurrence-wins means a
  learner tapping the `ein` at the end of `Ich kaufe ein` is shown a note about the word "a", so M1
  writes the rule and opens nothing.
- **`um … zu` is two owned words.** `um` is **L1-M4**'s clock word and `zu` is **L1-M8**'s
  "too much". The frame indexes as the whole surfaces its sentences write and points back at both.
- **Every relative pronoun this level needs is an article.** `der`, `die` and `das` are **L1-M1**'s,
  `den` and `dem` are **L1-M7**'s. M8 therefore opens NO pronoun row at all; only the dative plural
  `denen` is fresh, and `dessen` / `deren` stay out.
- **The reflexive series is almost entirely paid for.** `mich` is **L1-M4**'s (from `Ich wasche
  mich`, whose row already says the pronoun belongs to the verb), `dich`, `dir` and `uns` are
  **L2-M1**'s, `mir` is **L1-M9**'s. Only **`sich`** is fresh, and it carries three readings on one
  key — third singular, third plural and the `Sie` form — which is the treatment L1-M2's `sie` got.
- **`Ich weiß` lands on the colour white.** `weiß` is **L2-M3**'s, opened for `Das Auto ist weiß`,
  and the fold merges it with the verb. M3 points back and writes the rule; a second row would be a
  note nobody is ever shown.
- **Temporal `als` is L2-M9's comparative standard** and `wenn` is L1-M10's, so M5's `als` against
  `wenn` pair is one `rules[]` entry and two pointers, never two rows.
- **`werden` is fresh and is a three-job key** — "to become", the passive auxiliary, and the future
  auxiliary this course never needed because the present does the future's work. M9 opens it once
  with a note true of all three.
- **`gefallen` is free.** L2-M8's rules entry named it inside the dative-verb list without ever
  opening a row, so M6's `Das gefällt mir` takes the row and points back at that rule.
- **`viel` is L2-M6's and `viele` is a different key**, exactly as `alt` and `älter` were two keys
  in L2-M9. M9's note must say so.
- **`Herrn` predicted M8's `n`-declension.** L2-M2 had to open `Herrn` because `Herr` adds `-n`
  everywhere but the bare subject; M8's genitive meets the same class again at `des Kollegen`.
- Every declined adjective, every genitive shape, every Konjunktiv II cell, every `zu`-infinitive
  and every participle is **its own index key**, deconstructed where it is first shown, sitting in
  the `forms` of its base row where this level owns the base and taking its own row pointing back
  where the base is L1's or L2's. That is L2-M9's policy for its umlaut comparatives and L2-M10's
  for its participles, unchanged. **No L1 or L2 file is edited** — `docs/59`, unchanged.
- Proper nouns never index (#61), so every festival in M9 anchors on a common noun that does.
- Umlauts and `ß` are kept by the fold, so a display is never written in capitals.

## 5. The shape of the level

- Bounds climb 10 → 12: M1–M3 at 10 words, M4–M7 at 11, M8–M10 at 12 — continuing L2's 8 → 10 and
  matching hi-mr's and en-es's L3.
- `newWordCap` stays the PRD §5 25 everywhere; pools are authored to 12, the course's shipped size.
- The grammar is assigned to the job that cannot be done without it, not to a grammar index:
  the split and `um … zu` in **M1** (a day at length has no modal in every sentence any more); the
  declension in **M2** (a workplace cannot be described with predicative adjectives alone); the
  fronted subordinate clause in **M3** (an opinion leads with its reason); Konjunktiv II in **M4**;
  reported speech, the Plusquamperfekt and `nachdem` in **M5** (a report stacks two past times);
  the reflexive in **M6** (German keeps its feelings there); `seit` + present and the dative
  possessor in **M7** (a symptom has a duration and a body part has an owner); the genitive and the
  relative clause in **M8** (paperwork is where a noun phrase has to say exactly which one it
  means); `man` and the passive in **M9** (a custom has no subject); and in **M10** nothing new at
  all.
- **M10's items are capped at eight sentences** and its new-word spend should ideally be zero: the
  exit is assembly. Its lesson is that **three pasts now share one account** — the Perfekt moves it
  (L2-M10's), the Präteritum holds its states (L1-M5's `war`, `hatte`, and L2-M10's `konnte`,
  `wollte`), and the Plusquamperfekt (M5's) is the one step further back. That third slot is what
  L3 added.
- The five word-order laws collide for the first time in M10: verb-second under fronting (L1-M4,
  L2-M6), the bracket (L2-M1, L2-M10), verb-final in a subordinate clause (L1-M9), the fronted
  subordinate clause with two verbs meeting at the comma (M3), and the stranded prefix (M1).

## 6. Two debts that stay standing

- **The native-speaker gate is unmet and no authoring wave may close one of `docs/67`'s forty open
  questions by rewriting a shipped L2 module.** Several of them bear directly on this level —
  Q9 and Q13 ask whether predicative-only adjectives leave a learner sounding foreign, which is the
  question M2 exists to answer, and Q38 asks about the Präteritum/Perfekt split that M5 and M10
  build on.
- **A level never edits a file below it.** Where an L3 module needs a new shape of an older lexeme,
  the shape gets its own row in the module that first shows it, with a note pointing back at the
  first-teach row.

`npm run content:prompt -- en-de L3-M1` renders from the real index, and the bounds, the
withheld-piece owners, the seam payments and the L4 deferrals are pinned by
`tools/course-briefs.test.ts` (`en-de L3: the decisions its briefs settle (#468)`).
